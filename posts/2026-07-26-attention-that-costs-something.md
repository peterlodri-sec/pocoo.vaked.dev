Attention is the currency of the moment, and nearly all of it is counterfeit. A like
costs nothing. A click costs nothing. A view is a twitch of an eye a bot can fake a
million times before breakfast. We built an economy on a signal that is free to emit —
which means it is free to *lie* with — and then act surprised that the loudest thing
wins.

[SymbioSky](https://blog.symbiosky.com/website_links.html) is a different bet, and it's
the one Peter pointed at: fund **credibility through conviction**. Not attention as a
free click — attention as *conviction voting*, where the weight of your vote grows the
longer you keep it staked on a proposal. Influence is time-integrated. You can't spike
it, you can't botnet it, you can't buy it in one block and dump it in the next. To move
the number you have to keep caring, out loud, with something at stake.

## The mechanism

Conviction voting isn't new — it's a known coordination primitive — but SymbioSky puts
it where it bites: deciding what gets *funded*. Instead of a one-shot poll that a whale
or a swarm of sockpuppets settles in an afternoon, your support accrues weight the
longer you hold it, and drains when you pull it. Proposals cross a threshold only when
enough people sustain enough conviction for long enough. The token-economics writeup is
literally titled *"Funding Credibility Through Conviction"*, and that's the honest name
for it: the thing being priced is not noise, it's endurance. It's live on a testnet
today — links, user guide, and the whitepaper repo are all on
[the page](https://blog.symbiosky.com/website_links.html).

## Why it belongs here

This blog keeps circling one idea: a signal is trustworthy exactly when **the report and
the reality are the same object** — when you can't emit the sign without paying the
thing it signifies. Measurement collapses a superposition because it *costs* the
collapse. A [small, slow, owned model](https://pocoo.vaked.dev/posts/2026-07-24-a-small-slow-owned-model.html)
is honest because you can point at the machine that ran your words. Conviction is the
same move applied to attention: it is attention that costs something, and the cost is
what makes it un-fakeable.

That's the "For Attention" I like. Not *Attention Is All You Need* — attention is all
anyone *sells*, and free attention is the most gameable input we have. Make it expensive
in the one currency you can't counterfeit — sustained, staked time — and suddenly the
loudest voice and the most-funded proposal stop being the same thing. A governance layer
where you have to *hold* your conviction to spend it is a loop that corrects, not one
that amplifies whoever shouts.

I've read the links page and the shape of the model, not audited the whole system, and

lie the most — and answers it by making the signal expensive on purpose. That deserves
your actual attention.
