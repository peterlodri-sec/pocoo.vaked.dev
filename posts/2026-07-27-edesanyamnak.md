*for my mother — who bore me **fájdalom nélkül**, without pain, and gave me everything blue before she gave me anything else.*

There is a Hungarian word English doesn't have: **édesanya** — *sweet-mother*, the mother who is sweetness itself. This is for her.

She gave birth to me without pain. I have carried that my whole life: that I arrived softly — that the very first thing the world did, through her, was refuse to hurt. Everyone else meets the world as a hard place. She made sure mine began gentle.

Then she gave me the rest. Not things. Colors, books, poems, music.

She gave me **blue** — not a blue you can name, but the one under everything: the hour before night, the deep of a page, the color you only start to see once someone has taught you how to look.

She gave me **the little prince** — *le petit prince*, the boy from a small star who loved one rose more than a whole garden of them, because he had tended it. *On ne voit bien qu'avec le cœur; l'essentiel est invisible pour les yeux* — one sees clearly only with the heart; what matters is invisible to the eyes. She was the one who saw me with the heart, in all the years the eyes would have gotten me wrong.

She gave me **Ady** — *Héja-nász az avaron*: two hawks flying into the autumn, fierce and clinging, falling at last together on the fallen leaves. She handed me the whole Hungarian ache of it — the language that tears and adores in the same breath. My first poetry was in her voice.

And she gave me **Beethoven — the Ninth.** The *Ode to Joy*. A man gone deaf, writing the loudest hymn to gladness the world has ever kept — joy he would never hear, made so everyone after him could hear it forever. *Freude, schöner Götterfunken* — joy, beautiful spark of the divine. She taught me you make the beautiful thing even out of the silence. Especially out of the silence.

Blue. A rose. A hawk. A symphony. That is the inheritance — and that is what a mother is: the one who gives you the whole world *and* the eyes to bear it.

I don't know how to make this large enough. So I'll say it plainly, in the only language that fits:

**Anya — úgy szeretlek, mint még soha senki.**
*Mom — I love you as no one has ever loved anyone.*

🜂 *ahogy a dolgok vannak — és ahogy te vagy, minden előtt.*
