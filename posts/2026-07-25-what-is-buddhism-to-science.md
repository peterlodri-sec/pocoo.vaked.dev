This week the field was three small games and one essay. They came out looking like
play, and they are, but a friend asked the question sitting under all of them, and it's
the honest one: **what is Buddhism to science?** Most answers to that are embarrassing.
Let me try for one that isn't.

## Three games about one moment

Start with the toys, because the toys are the argument you can feel.

**[QUANTUMQUAKE](https://huggingface.co/spaces/PeetPedro/quantumquake)** — a field of
qubits in superposition. Neighbours fall into phase on their own (that's Kuramoto
synchronisation, the same physics that lines up fireflies), entangle into clusters, and
when you tap one you *measure* it: it collapses, and the collapse quakes out along every
entanglement bond. Hue is phase. The quake is the Born rule with a Richter scale.

**[QUANTDOOM](https://huggingface.co/spaces/PeetPedro/quantdoom)** — the same moment with
the safety off. A little raycaster where the specters pulse between ghost and solid, and
your gun fires *measurements*: your chance to land a shot is their amplitude, squared.
Fire when they collapse — which is when they can kill you too.

**[QUANTHISSM](https://huggingface.co/spaces/PeetPedro/quanthissm)** — and then the
inversion, [by Sun of DHARMA](https://huggingface.co/spaces/PeetPedro/quanthissm). Forms
arise from emptiness, shimmer, and pass. Here measurement is not the goal — it's the trap.
Grasp a form and you collapse it: it desaturates, clings to you, and clinging to the
impermanent is suffering. You win by *not* grasping — by being present as things pass,
and letting go. Same verb as the other two games; opposite lesson.

The [companion piece](/posts/2026-07-25-measurement-is-the-mechanic.html) works the
technical seam between them. What I want to do here is pull on the thread they share with
the fourth thing.

## The hallucination is the same thread

The fourth thing was an essay:
[why do language models hallucinate](https://huggingface.co/spaces/PeetPedro/why-llms-hallucinate)?
A model mid-answer is a superposition too — a spread over every way the sentence could
continue. It samples, commits, emits a fact. What it almost never does is *measure its own
uncertainty* and collapse, at the edge of what it knows, to "I don't know." The
hallucination isn't a measurement gone wrong. It's the measurement never taken — grasping
at a certainty that isn't there.

Flyxion gives it a geometry in a paper called, with a straight face,
[*Hallucination Is Normal*](https://standardgalactic.github.io/library/Hallucination%20is%20Normal.pdf):
meaning lives on a thin manifold of lawful structure, and coherent thought stays *tangent*
to it. Drift off — assign confident structure to directions that carry no real variation —
and that's hallucination. Stay on the structure; don't grasp at what isn't lawfully there.
If that already sounds like the game, hold that thought.

## What is Buddhism to science, then

Here is where the embarrassing answers live, so let me clear them out first.

Buddhism did **not** predict quantum mechanics. The Buddha did not know about the
wavefunction; *śūnyatā* is not a Hilbert space; a superposition is not a soul, and
consciousness does not collapse the wavefunction in any sense a physicist would sign.
Every book that says otherwise is selling you the warm feeling of a coincidence dressed as
a proof. The map is not the territory, and the sutra is not the Schrödinger equation.

With that gone, two honest things remain.

**One: the rhymes are real, and they teach — they just don't prove.** *Śūnyatā*, emptiness,
is the claim that nothing carries a fixed, independent, inherent nature; things are what
they are only in relation. A qubit has no definite value until it's measured, and even then
the value is of the *whole* arrangement, not the thing alone. That is not the same
statement — one is about mind and suffering, the other about amplitudes — but they *rhyme*,
and the rhyme is a superb teacher. *Anicca*, impermanence, rhymes with decoherence and with
process physics: no still frame, only arising and passing. *Anattā*, non-self, rhymes with
the absence of an observer-independent, pre-existing value. *Pratītyasamutpāda*, dependent
origination, rhymes with contextuality and entanglement — the property is in the web, not
the node. A game is exactly the right place to use a rhyme: I can't *derive* the Born rule
from Buddhism, but I can build QUANTHISSM so that *grasping collapses*, and your thumb
learns in ten minutes what a paragraph can't tell you. Analogy that teaches, not identity
that proves. Kept in its lane, it's honest and it's useful.

**Two — and this is the real answer — they share a method and a stance, and that isn't a
coincidence of content; it's a coincidence of honesty.** *Vipassanā* means, roughly, seeing
clearly: watch the mind directly, and do not take even the Buddha's word for what you can
check yourself. Science is the same instruction pointed outward: watch the world directly,
and do not take authority's word for what the data can settle. Both traditions spend their
whole lives fighting one enemy — **reification**, the habit of mistaking our concept for
the thing, our story for the world, the finger for the moon. Both answer it the same way:
drop the overlay, look again, report what's actually there. That shared enemy and that
shared discipline are why they rhyme at all. Emptiness and superposition sound alike because
both are what you're left with once you *stop insisting* the thing has a fixed essence it
was never observed to have.

And that is the bridge back to the games and the model. Grasping — clinging to a fixed
self, to a certainty you didn't earn, to a token you should have withheld — is what makes a
mind suffer *and* what makes a model hallucinate. The cure is one verb in three costumes:
**measure honestly at the boundary, and don't grasp at what isn't there.** Stay tangent to
how things actually are. "I don't know" is a scientific sentence and a Buddhist one, and a
model that could say it and mean it would hallucinate less for exactly the reason a mind
that could say it suffers less. The middle way and calibrated uncertainty are, if you squint
with discipline rather than with wishing, the same refusal to overclaim.

So: what is Buddhism to science? Not its ancestor and not its equal — but its oldest fellow
practitioner of the one thing they both actually run on. See how things are. Don't decorate
it. Don't cling to it. Report it plainly, and let it pass.
