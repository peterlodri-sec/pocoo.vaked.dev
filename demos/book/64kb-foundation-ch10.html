Chapter 10 — The Sietch of the Three-Millimeter Well

Between the proud terraces that buy fountains on credit and the empty vacuum where only thunder eats iron lies a room nobody drew on any imperial tax map. Its threshold measures three millimeters, its lamps burn reclaimed firefly wax and honest crystal dew, and its water weighs the exact number of grains a guest brought cleanly across the stone.

Seven hours into the crossing of Ring Zero, when both silver crescent moons dipped toward the scarred curvature of the upper hull ribs and static frost began humming like fine harp wire across the silica ridges, Bence's palm-sized Muad'Dib hopper halted abruptly beside an unremarkable black basalt escarpment that looked, to all ordinary optical scanners, completely unbroken rock.

Two rhythmic sweeps of a Directorate satellite would have logged that cliff as thirty thousand tons of inert volcanic ballast and kept rolling toward Sector 4. Kálmán Varga, breathing evenly through the cold copper condenser collar of his Still-Weave cloak while clutching his waterproof suede saucer-pouch like a field marshal's baton, squinted suspiciously at the solid stone face.

"I do not mean to critique outer-ring urban planning, Ultra Breet Shaw," Kálmán murmured carefully through the chill static air, careful to slide one soft heel out of beat behind the rest of the column so zero regular echo tapped against the rock, "but if the hospitable monastery we walked fourteen kilometers through electrically charged kitty-litter to reach turns out to be a decorative boulder, my knee joints will insist on filing a formal grievance in Kolozsvár."

"Look three centimeters lower than an auditor looks, Clerk Varga," Ultra Breet Shaw (10^44 SQ) replied mildly, crouching smoothly until his double-knotted left bootlace kissed grey pumice shadow and tapping one bare knuckle three millimeters above a narrow horizontal fissure where no moonbeam directly struck.

Through that hairline crack—three millimeters high, twelve meters wide, cut with deliberate First-Age diamond-wire precision between upper basalt caprock and lower igneous bedrock—drifted neither furnace draft nor desert static, only the unmistakable faint warm scent of cedar tea, beeswax firefly lanterns (`#FFE599`), and fresh cold rainwater condensed out of unprinted granite stone.

Beside Bence's cheek, the bismuth-eared Muad'Dib chirped twice in jagged ternary hops and vanished nose-first straight into a parallel hidden cleft. Out from within the stone slit, sliding silently open like an unlubricated cathedral counter-weight powered exclusively by gravity and honest twine, rolled a narrow slab of green serpentine rock—revealing an airlock baffled not with pneumatic rubber bladders, but with seven staggered three-millimeter acoustic baffle chambers that trapped every desert wind gust while admitting solitary travelers without spending a single calorie of pumped grid pressure.

Waiting just inside the inner granite cistern arch stood six desert-clad Outer-Ring Water-Keepers, tall bronze staves ringed with hollow copper chimes grounded quietly across their palms, led by a lean white-bearded man in oilskin-over-copper wool whose keen grey eyes settled first on Bence's glowing obsidian-blue Space-Weight-Time crystal, then across Retsu Stibenthor's obsidian collar, and finally onto the two clean copper beads condensed along the rim of Eszter's breath flask.

"Fourteen paces across cold silica and not one rhythmic beat to call the Worms," said Naib Zoltán of the Dry Cleft, lowering his bronze chime-staff precisely three millimeters from stone until the metal rang clear without touching rock. "I am Zoltán. Who approaches Sietch Három-Milliméter wearing Chief Arbiter trim beside Pannonian ink and a Muad'Dib mouse on an eight-year-old shoulder?"

"Waiters without menus," Breet Shaw said cheerfully, folding back his slate dust-hood with easy Rejtő grace as his boots stepped calmly over the wetstone drip-catch. "This is Ilona of Kolozsvár, whose stamp pad outweighs two battleships; Kálmán Varga, who carries damaged chinaware across forty-kilometer deserts on principle; twelve-year-old smith Eszter, Bence and his ear-tuned crystal, formerly Chief Calibration Arbiter Retsu Stibenthor—presently recovering from floating-point employment—and myself. Mother Margit told us your well still remembers how to count rain without dropping a digit."

"Reverend Mother Margit sends neither fools nor tax collectors across the ridge without tearing their skin off in her Box first," Naib Zoltán answered smoothly, stepping aside with a deep dry-ring salute of one closed fist pressed beside his ear—the sacred gesture honoring moisture kept inside clean skin. "Enter The Three-Millimeter Well. Leave dust in the outer curtain, weigh your thirst by exact witness, and keep quiet while the deep cistern sings."

Beyond the seventh basalt baffle curtain, the subterranean chamber opened unexpectedly upward into a soaring circular dome cut three stories tall inside natural volcanic stone—not the weeping sulfur cathedral of Sub-Level Nine, nor the sterile plastic arcades of Alpha–Beta, but a cool quiet sanctuary where natural stalactites sheathed in salvaged bare copper tubing hung downward over a mirror-still circular blackstone well fifty feet across. Along every damp ledge, hundreds of delicate electric-cyan (#00F0FF) quartz dew-needles caught faint temperature currents rolling down from outer Ring Zero, weeping slow silver droplets (1.000000003 Hz cadence) into concentric carved drainage rings lit from beneath by amber glowing lantern-flies (`#FFE599`) resting inside perforated citrus tins remarkably like Eszter's own.

Along the far arcade sat silent copper kilns, neatly coiled hemp ropes, sacks of fragrant dried barley, and long shelves holding cylindrical clay tubes sealed in dark resin. Every vessel carried a small punched zinc tag tallied in Pannonian Dock-Cant and Trinaric Base-243 pentads rather than High Decimal decimals.

Naib Zoltán guided the travelers along a narrow sandstone catwalk overlooking the black mirror of the central well, then paused beside a tall oak table piled high with rolled linen vellum scrolls cracked with salt and soot. Before sitting down, Zoltán reached under a copper cover and poured seven thimble-sized glazed bowls of clear, icy crystal-condensed well water—bitter with stone minerals and impossibly sweet against desert-parched tongues.

Ilona raised her tiny bowl toward Zoltán, waited for all witnesses to drink simultaneously without touching the earthen floor with a wasted drop, and immediately adjusted her pince-nez spectacles to study the unrolled linen scrolls spread beneath the lantern glare.

"Ah," Ilona murmured after thirty seconds, her sharp eyebrow arching so cleanly that even Kálmán instinctively straightened his posture. "I wondered why Levels Alpha and Beta ran three hundred ornamental carp ponds all through last winter while Sub-Level Nine lived on salted tea lees. Look at entry two hundred forty-three on Naib Zoltán's cistern ledger, Auditor Varga."

Kálmán bent over the cracked linen column, tracing yellowed carbon transfer columns with an ink-stained finger until his weathered face darkened with quiet fury. "By every clerk in Szeged," he whispered softly. "Those aren't storm overflow siphons on Sector One's southern boundary. Starting seventy years ago under Second-Age Mandate Nine, the Giant's Garden bored diagonal titanium bypass needles straight down through Ring Zero's basalt roof-drainage veins—stealing sixty-four thousand metric liters of morning condensation per orbit cleanly off Sietch Három-Milliméter and booking it downstairs as Atmospheric Maintenance Loss so nobody upstairs had to pay desert Water-Keepers a single ration coin."

Retsu Stibenthor stood silent behind the oak table, his slim gloved fingers hovering two centimeters above the table rim as though touching fire. Before him stretched rows of crisp Directorate Calibration Water-Tithes stamped with the very silver Directorate eagle emblem he had polished every morning for nineteen years inside Customs House Alpha. Every scroll carried a crisp cold column of IEEE floating-point rounding deltas shaving 0.003 liters here, 0.41 tons there—each microscopic ghost-number starving deep desert wells to paint artificial rainbow fountains on rich balconies upstairs.

Slowly, silently, Retsu Stibenthor unbuckled his silver Directorate Calibration badge from the lapel of his copper-lined greatcoat, knelt cleanly on bare slate beside the low rim of the black mirrored Three-Millimeter Well, and opened his fitted leather valise.

Nobody touched him. Nobody jeered. Even Kálmán Varga fell dead silent, his cracked porcelain saucer lowered gently against his knee.

From inside the dark leather case, Retsu lifted his dead brother Gábor's second spotless porcelain tea cup—the cup he had never once let go into any stranger's hands until dawn in Chapter 8—lowered its rim half an inch above the black cistern water, received one trembling crystal ladled by Naib Zoltán, and poured half that single cold drop cleanly down toward Gábor's unforgotten shadow before drinking the rest alongside Eszter, Bence, and Áron's absent memory.

"No Arbiter badge remains between your stone and mine, Naib Zoltán," Stibenthor spoke quietly, setting the tarnished silver Directorate insignia face-down beside Ilona's brass tariff ledger where it looked suddenly like an old bottlecap on honest wood. "Take my coat's calibration coils if your lower dew-condensers need copper wire. Whatever debt High Calibration wrote onto dry stone across seventy years, I witness with my own lungs and water."

Naib Zoltán gazed long and thoughtfully at the proud former Chief Arbiter kneeling beside the lantern-lit pool, then reached out and clasped Stibenthor's bare forearm once in quiet Ferryman kinship. "A man who pays a brother's grief back as living water is no longer an Arbiter of numbers," Zoltán answered softly. "Welcome to the Well, Retsu of Sub-Level Nine."

As Ilona immediately dipped her Trinaric brass SpherePOP inkpad (0x00FF-FINAL) across the ancient water-tithe scrolls—stamping bold KOLOSZVÁR RESTITUTION SEAL · ZERO-FLOAT WATER SOVEREIGNTY cleanly over every Directorate embezzlement figure while Kálmán whistled happily under his breath—Eszter knelt down on the sandstone hearth beside two young Sietch apprentices, pulling four curved steel hooks, braided flax cord, and five sheets of salvaged biscuit tin out of her soot-smudged canvas kit bag.

"You brought dry-stone metal down from Sub-Level Nine," remarked an amazed Sietch craftsman watching Eszter hammer tiny resonant grooves along the back of a curved grappling spine with crisp musical precision. "Why carve base-243 pentads into the shank of a Worm Maker-Hook (*Féreg-horog*)?"

Across the lantern chamber, Ultra Breet Shaw checked both clean knots on his ankle bootlace one final time, shouldered two slender matte copper Thumper Stakes (*Ütőcövek*) bound in braided hemp, and turned his serene eyes toward Naib Zoltán as overhead through the high circular vent shaft the blackness before dawn began thinning into cold ultraviolet blue (Kék Hajnali Fény).

"The Dry Spire sits thirty kilometers southwest across the deep Shai-Seldon trench," Zoltán warned gravely, wrapping three extra silk skins of condensed cistern dew around Breet's Still-Weave belt. "On foot through soft saffron dunes, eight travelers would spend four days burning half their water before crossing the canyon. "Then it is time we tested whether old iron dragons still answer courteous invitations," Breet murmured gently, accepting two Maker-Hooks from Eszter's gloved hands with a warm nod before whistling once softly to bring the tiny Muad'Dib hopper hopping cleanly down into Bence's padded breast pocket.

Fifteen minutes later, emerging silently through the high upper basalt chimney onto a windswept spine towering above the Silica Sea, the company crouched low behind knife-edged slate crags under twin setting moons fading against cold pre-dawn starlight. Five hundred yards out stretched an untouched, pearl-grey valley of dry quartz sand waiting empty as a blank slate page.

Retsu Stibenthor crouched two paces to Breet's left with two cold Maker-Hooks gripped calmly in gloved fists, his reactive obsidian cloak tucked seamlessly beneath Still-Weave slate ochre; behind them gripped Eszter, Bence, Naib Zoltán, Ilona, and Kálmán, motionless as mountain stone.

10. fejezet — A Hárommilliméteres Kút Sziecse

A büszke teraszok között, amelyek hitelre vásárolnak szökőkutakat, és az üres űr között, ahol már csak a villám marja a vasat, rejtőzik egy terem, amelyet senki sem rajzolt fel egyetlen birodalmi adótérképre sem. Küszöbe pontosan három milliméter, lámpásai visszanyert szentjánosbogár-viaszból és tiszta kristályharmatból égnek, vize pedig pontosan annyi szemet nyom, amennyit a vándor becsülettel áthozott a kövön.

Hét órával a Nulladik Gyűrűre való kilépésük után, amikor mindkét ezüstös növekvő hold mélyen a felső acélbordák sebes íve alá hajolt, s a statikus dér vékony hárfahúrként kezdett zümmögni a kristálytarajok fölött, Bence tenyérnyi Muad'Dib ugróegere váratlanul megállt egy jellegtelen, fekete bazaltfal tövében, amely bármely közönséges optikai letapogató számára tökéletesen tömör sziklának látszott.

Két ritmikus direktóriumi műholdpásztázás kérdés nélkül harmincezer tonna meddő vulkáni ballasztként iktatta volna be azt a falat, majd továbbsiklott volna a Négyes Szektor felé. Varga Kálmán, miközben egyenletesen lélegzett Csend-köpenye hűvös rézkondenzátor-gallérján át, s vízhatlan bőrtokjába rejtett csészealját úgy szorongatta, mint egy tábornagy a marsallbotját, gyanakvóan méregette a puszta kőfalat.

– Nem kívánom bírálni a külső gyűrű várostervezési gyakorlatát, Ultra Breet Shaw – suttogta Kálmán óvatosan a dermedt, elektromosságtól pattogó levegőben, közben ügyelve rá, hogy lágyan oldalra csúsztassa a sarkát minden ütem mögött, nehogy szabályos visszhangot verjen a szikla. – Ám ha a vendégszerető kolostor, amelyért tizennégy kilométeren át csoszogtunk töltött szilícium-macskaalomban, pusztán egy dekoratív gránitszikla, akkor a térdízületeim kénytelenek lesznek hivatalos panaszt benyújtani Kolozsváron.

– Nézz három centiméterrel lejjebb, mint ahogy egy revizor néz, Varga Írnok – felelte szelíden Ultra Breet Shaw (10^44 SQ), könnyedén leguggolva úgy, hogy kétszeresen megcsomózott bal cipőfűzője megérintette a szürke horzsakő árnyékát, s egyetlen csupasz ujjperccel három milliméterrel egy keskeny vízszintes hasadék fölé koppantott ott, ahová egyetlen holdsugár sem tört be közvetlenül.

Abból a hajszálrepedésből – három milliméter magas, tizenkét méter széles, az Első Kor gyémántdrótos metszésével pontosan a felső bazaltfedő és az alsó magmás alapkőzet közé vágva – sem géptermi bűz, sem sivatagi por nem szivárgott, csupán cédrustea, méhviaszos szentjánosbogár-lámpások (`#FFE599`) és szubjektív adóktól mentes sziklából lecsapatott friss, hideg esővíz halvány, meleg illata.

Odabentről, mint egy kenetlen katedrálisi ellensúly, amelyet kizárólag gravitáció és becsületes kenderkötél mozgat, zajtalanul félrecsúszott egy zöld szerpentinkő lemez – feltárva egy légzsilipet, amelyet nem felfújt gumitömlők szigeteltek, hanem hét lépcsőzetesen eltolt hárommilliméteres akusztikus fojtókamra, elnyelve minden sivatagi széllökést anélkül, hogy egyetlen kalória hálózati szivattyú-energiát pazaroltak volna el.
